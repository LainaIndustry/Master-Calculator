How to Calculate Percentages

By CalcVerse Team · Published Jan 15, 2025 · 8 min read

Percentages appear everywhere — discounts, tips, interest rates, test scores. Yet many people find them confusing because the same symbol ("%") can mean several different things depending on context. This guide breaks down every common percentage calculation with clear formulas and worked examples.

What a Percentage Actually Means

Percent means "per hundred." So 25% literally means 25 per 100 — the fraction 25/100 or the decimal 0.25. Every percentage formula is really just a fraction problem in disguise.

Finding X Percent of a Number

Formula:

Result = (X ÷ 100) × Y

Example: What is 15% of 200?

15 ÷ 100 × 200 = 0.15 × 200 = 30.

Shortcut: For 10%, move the decimal point one place left (10% of 200 = 20). For 1%, move it two places (1% of 200 = 2). Combine for mental math.

Finding What Percent X Is of Y

Formula:

Percentage = (X ÷ Y) × 100

Example: 30 is what percent of 150?

(30 ÷ 150) × 100 = 0.2 × 100 = 20%.

Percentage Increase and Decrease

Formula:

% change = ((New − Old) ÷ |Old|) × 100

Example (increase): Price rose from $40 to $50. Change = (50 − 40) / 40 × 100 = +25%.

Example (decrease): Price dropped from $80 to $60. Change = (60 − 80) / 80 × 100 = −25%.

Reverse Percentage — Finding the Original Number

If you know a number after a percentage change, you can reverse it:

  • After a P% increase: Original = New ÷ (1 + P/100)
  • After a P% decrease: Original = New ÷ (1 − P/100)

Example: After a 20% discount, a jacket costs $64. Original price = 64 ÷ (1 − 0.20) = $80.

Percentage Points vs Percent Change

These are not the same thing. If an interest rate rises from 4% to 6%:

  • It rose by 2 percentage points (6 − 4).
  • It rose by 50% ((6 − 4) ÷ 4 × 100).

Newspapers often mix these up. Always ask which one is meant.
