How Extra Payments Help

Every extra dollar you pay goes straight to principal. Reducing the balance means less interest accrues next month, which means more of your next payment goes to principal, and so on. This compounding effect is why even small extra payments save thousands over a mortgage's life.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your current loan balance.
  2. Enter your interest rate.
  3. Enter the remaining term in years.
  4. Enter any extra monthly payment you plan to make.
  5. Click Calculate.

Formula

Base monthly payment: M = P × [r(1+r)ⁿ] ÷ [(1+r)ⁿ − 1]

With extra: simulate the loan month by month applying M + extra to the balance until it hits zero.

Worked Example

$250,000 mortgage at 6.5% for 30 years:

  • Base payment: $1,580.17/month
  • Extra: $200/month
  • New payoff time: ~24 years, 2 months (vs 30 years)
  • Interest saved: ~$130,000

Understanding Your Result

The result shows your new monthly payment and how much faster the loan will be repaid. The detail box shows the exact interest savings and time saved — the two numbers that matter most.

Common Mistakes

  • Forgetting to earmark extra payments as principal-only. Without this, some lenders apply them to future scheduled payments, delaying rather than reducing interest.
  • Prioritizing mortgage prepayment over higher-interest debt — credit cards at 20% should come first.
  • Depleting emergency savings to make extra payments.
  • Ignoring prepayment penalties (rare but real).
