What Is a Percentage Calculator?

A percentage calculator is a tool that converts between ratios, fractions, and the "parts per hundred" notation. It answers everyday questions like:

  • What is 15% of 200?
  • 30 is what percent of 150?
  • What is the percentage increase from 40 to 50?

How to Use the Percentage Calculator

  1. Choose the right mode. "X% of Y" for parts of a whole; "X is what % of Y" for ratios; "old → new" for change.
  2. Enter your numbers in the fields. Decimals are allowed.
  3. Click Calculate (or simply type — results update live).
  4. Read the result and formula shown in the result box.
  5. Use Reset to clear the fields and start over.

Percentage Formula

The core formula is:

Percentage value = (Part ÷ Whole) × 100

For "X% of Y":

Result = (X ÷ 100) × Y

For percentage change:

% change = ((New − Old) ÷ |Old|) × 100

How the Calculation Works

A percentage is just a fraction with denominator 100. When you say "15%", you mean "15 per 100" = 0.15. Multiplying a value by 0.15 returns the portion. To reverse the process — e.g., "30 is what percent of 150?" — divide the part by the whole and multiply by 100 to convert the fraction back to a percentage.

Example Calculation

Problem: A shirt costs $40 and is now on sale for $50 after a price increase. What is the percentage change?

Old = 40, New = 50. Change = (50 − 40) ÷ |40| × 100 = 10 ÷ 40 × 100 = 25% increase.

Problem 2: What is 18% of 250?

18 ÷ 100 × 250 = 0.18 × 250 = 45.

Understanding Your Result

The result is a number in the units of the input (for "X% of Y"), a percentage (for "X is what % of Y"), or a signed percentage (for change). Positive change means an increase, negative means a decrease. If the denominator is 0, the result is undefined — the calculator will tell you.

Common Mistakes

  • Confusing percentage points with percent change. A change from 4% to 6% is 2 percentage points but a 50% increase.
  • Using the new value as the denominator in a change formula. Always use the original (old) value.
  • Forgetting to divide by 100. "15%" is 0.15, not 15.
  • Dividing by zero. This produces an undefined result — always check your denominator.
