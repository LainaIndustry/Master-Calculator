What Is the Pythagorean Theorem?

In any right triangle, the square of the hypotenuse (the side opposite the right angle) equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides:

a² + b² = c²

How to Use It

  1. Enter any two known sides.
  2. Leave the unknown blank.
  3. Results appear as you type.

The Three Rearrangements

  • Find hypotenuse: c = √(a² + b²)
  • Find leg a: a = √(c² − b²)
  • Find leg b: b = √(c² − a²)

Worked Example

Legs 3 and 4:

  • c = √(3² + 4²) = √(9 + 16) = √25 = 5
  • Area = ½ × 3 × 4 = 6
  • Perimeter = 3 + 4 + 5 = 12

This is the famous 3-4-5 triangle, one of many "Pythagorean triples" with integer sides.

Common Pythagorean Triples

  • 3, 4, 5
  • 5, 12, 13
  • 8, 15, 17
  • 7, 24, 25
  • 20, 21, 29

Any multiple of a triple is also a triple (e.g. 6, 8, 10).

Understanding Your Result

The theorem only works for right triangles (one 90° angle). If your triangle doesn't have a right angle, use the Law of Cosines instead.

Common Mistakes

  • Mixing up which side is the hypotenuse — it's always the longest and opposite the right angle.
  • Entering values where c ≤ a or c ≤ b (impossible for a right triangle).
  • Using this on non-right triangles.
  • Forgetting to take the square root at the end.
