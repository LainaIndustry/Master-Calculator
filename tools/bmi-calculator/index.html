What Is BMI?

Body Mass Index (BMI) is a simple numeric measure that compares your weight to your height. It was developed in the 19th century by Adolphe Quetelet and is widely used by doctors, insurers, and public health agencies as a quick screening tool.

BMI does not measure body fat directly. Instead, it estimates whether your weight is in a healthy range for your height. It is useful at the population level but has important limitations for individuals.

How to Use the BMI Calculator

  2. Enter your weight and height.

BMI Formula

Metric:

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²

Imperial:

BMI = 703 × weight (lb) ÷ height (in)²

How the Calculation Works

BMI is your weight divided by the square of your height. Because height is squared, taller people have a much larger denominator — meaning a 6 ft person and a 5 ft person can weigh very differently and still have the same BMI. This is why BMI is a normalized measure.

Example Calculation

A person weighing 70 kg and 175 cm tall:

  • Height in meters: 1.75 m
  • Height squared: 3.0625
  • BMI = 70 ÷ 3.0625 ≈ 22.9 (normal weight)

Understanding Your Result

Your BMI number places you in a category. The "healthy weight range" shown is computed from the same formula rearranged: for a healthy BMI of 18.5–24.9, the healthy weight range is 18.5 × height² to 24.9 × height².

Common Mistakes

  • Assuming BMI measures body fat — it doesn't.
  • Using BMI for children (they need age- and sex-specific percentile charts).
  • Applying adult BMI categories to athletes, pregnant women, or the elderly.
  • Treating a single BMI reading as a diagnosis.
