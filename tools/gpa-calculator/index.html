What Is GPA?

GPA (Grade Point Average) is the standard measure of academic performance in US schools and universities. It converts letter grades into numbers and averages them, weighted by credit hours.

How to Use It

  1. Add one row per course.
  2. Enter the course name (optional).
  3. Select the letter grade you received.
  4. Enter the credit hours for that course.
  5. Click Calculate GPA.

Formula

GPA = Σ(grade points × credits) ÷ Σ(credits)

Worked Example

Three courses:

  • Math (3 cr, A = 4.0) → 12.0 points
  • English (4 cr, B+ = 3.3) → 13.2 points
  • History (3 cr, A− = 3.7) → 11.1 points
  • Total points = 36.3, total credits = 10
  • GPA = 36.3 / 10 = 3.63

Understanding Your Result

GPA ranges from 0.0 to 4.0 on the unweighted scale. Some schools offer "weighted" GPA where AP/Honors courses get a bonus point — check your school's policy.

Common Mistakes

  • Ignoring credit hours — a 4-credit A affects GPA more than a 1-credit A.
  • Mixing weighted and unweighted scales.
  • Using "+/−" grades when the school doesn't recognize them.
  • Not accounting for pass/fail courses (typically excluded).
