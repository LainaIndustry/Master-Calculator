What Is a Loan Calculator?

A loan calculator determines the fixed monthly payment needed to fully repay a loan over a given term at a given interest rate. It also shows how much of that payment goes to interest vs principal, and the total cost of borrowing.

How to Use the Loan Calculator

  1. Enter the loan amount (principal).
  2. Enter the annual interest rate as a percentage.
  3. Enter the term in years.
  4. Click Calculate. Your monthly payment, total paid, and total interest appear instantly, along with the amortization table.

Loan Payment Formula

M = P × [ r(1+r)^n ] ÷ [ (1+r)^n − 1 ]
  • M = monthly payment
  • P = principal (loan amount)
  • r = monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100)
  • n = total number of payments (years × 12)

How the Calculation Works

Each payment is fixed, but its composition shifts over time. Early payments are mostly interest; later payments are mostly principal. The formula above solves for the payment that makes the remaining balance exactly zero at the end of the term. Interest for each period is computed as balance × r, and the principal portion is M − interest.

Example Calculation

A $25,000 loan at 6.5% over 5 years:

  • r = 0.065 / 12 = 0.005417
  • n = 60
  • M = 25000 × [0.005417 × (1.005417)^60] ÷ [(1.005417)^60 − 1]
  • M ≈ $489.13/month
  • Total paid ≈ $29,347.80 — interest ≈ $4,347.80

Understanding Your Result

The monthly payment is the amount due each month. The amortization table shows how the balance drops over time. Notice how early rows have large interest and small principal — that's the nature of amortized loans. Making extra principal payments can dramatically reduce total interest.

Common Mistakes

  • Using the annual rate in the monthly formula. Always divide by 12.
  • Forgetting about fees, insurance, or taxes that some lenders include in "monthly payment".
  • Confusing APR with the nominal interest rate — APR includes fees.
  • Assuming a fixed rate when the loan has a variable rate.
