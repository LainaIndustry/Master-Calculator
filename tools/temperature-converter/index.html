What Is a Temperature Converter?

It converts a temperature from one scale to another. Unlike other unit conversions, temperature uses offset arithmetic — not just multiplication — because the scales have different zero points.

How to Use It

  1. Enter the temperature value.
  2. Pick the source scale.
  3. Pick the target scale.
  4. Results update instantly, showing all three scales.

The Formulas

°F = (°C × 9/5) + 32
°C = (°F − 32) × 5/9
K  = °C + 273.15
°C = K − 273.15

Why the Offset?

The Celsius and Fahrenheit scales set zero at different points. Celsius zeroes at water's freezing point; Fahrenheit zeroes at a historical low temperature. Kelvin zeroes at absolute zero — the theoretical point where all molecular motion stops.

Common Mistakes

  • Using a simple multiplier instead of the offset formula.
  • Forgetting that °C and K use the same degree size but different zero points.
  • Confusing "40° below zero" (ambient temperature) with absolute zero.
