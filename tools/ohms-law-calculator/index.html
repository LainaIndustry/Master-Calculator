What Is Ohm's Law?

Ohm's law states that the current through a conductor is directly proportional to voltage and inversely proportional to resistance: V = I × R. Combined with the power formula, it lets you solve for any electrical quantity if you know two others.

How to Use This Calculator

  3. Results update as you type.

The Four Formulas

  • V = I × R
  • I = V ÷ R
  • R = V ÷ I
  • P = V × I = I²R = V²/R

Example

A 12 V source driving a 24 Ω resistor:

  • I = 12 / 24 = 0.5 A
  • P = 12 × 0.5 = 6 W

Understanding Your Result

Every result is derived from exactly the two values you entered, using the standard formulas. Verify sign conventions and units (volts, amps, ohms, watts).

Common Mistakes

  • Mixing units — milliamps (mA) with amps (A), or kilohms (kΩ) with ohms (Ω).
  • Using this for AC circuits. Ohm's law applies to DC; AC uses impedance (Z), not resistance.
  • Assuming linearity for non-ohmic devices like diodes and LEDs.
  • Forgetting the resistor's power rating. Always choose a resistor rated above the computed P.
