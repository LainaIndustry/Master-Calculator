What Is BMR?

Basal Metabolic Rate is the number of calories your body burns at complete rest — just to keep your heart beating, lungs breathing, and cells functioning. It accounts for 60–70% of your total daily calorie burn.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Choose metric or imperial units.
  2. Enter age, sex, weight, and height.
  3. Pick your activity level.
  4. Choose a formula (Mifflin-St Jeor is more accurate for most people).
  5. Click Calculate.

The Formulas

Mifflin-St Jeor (recommended):

Men:   BMR = 10w + 6.25h − 5a + 5
Women: BMR = 10w + 6.25h − 5a − 161

Where w = weight (kg), h = height (cm), a = age (years).

Harris-Benedict (revised):

Men:   BMR = 88.362 + 13.397w + 4.799h − 5.677a
Women: BMR = 447.593 + 9.247w + 3.098h − 4.330a

Worked Example

A 30-year-old woman, 165 cm, 65 kg, moderately active:

  • BMR = 10×65 + 6.25×165 − 5×30 − 161 = 650 + 1031 − 150 − 161 = 1370 cal/day
  • TDEE = 1370 × 1.55 ≈ 2124 cal/day
  • Weight loss target: ~1624 cal/day (−500)
  • Weight gain target: ~2624 cal/day (+500)

Understanding Your Result

Your BMR is the absolute floor for calorie intake. Eating below it long-term is unsafe — it deprives your body of energy for basic functions. Use your TDEE as the maintenance target: eat below it to lose weight, above to gain.

Common Mistakes

  • Eating below your BMR to lose weight faster — dangerous and counterproductive.
  • Trusting BMR formulas as exact. Individual variation is ±10%.
  • Using "activity multiplier" too aggressively (most people overestimate).
  • Forgetting that BMR drops as you lose weight.
